# Stock phrasing to review

Use this list as a review aid, not a vocabulary ban. A word can stay when it is precise in context. When a phrase is empty or performative, replace it with the concrete point rather than a rarer synonym.

## Empty scene-setting

Review openings such as:

- "In today's..."
- "When it comes to..."
- "At its core..."
- "It's worth noting that..."
- "This is where X comes in."
- "Let's dive in."

Start with the subject, relevant situation or decision instead.

## Corporate promotion

Review language such as "unlock the power", "elevate", "supercharge", "move the needle", "game-changing", "seamless" and "thought leadership". Replace it with what the product, person or change actually does.

Prefer familiar words where they carry the same meaning: "use" rather than "utilise", and "start" rather than "commence". Keep established technical terms, including "robust", when they have a specific technical meaning.

## Formulaic transitions and closings

Review automatic transitions such as "Moreover", "Furthermore", "Additionally", "Overall" and "In conclusion". Use a transition only when it explains the relationship between ideas.

Remove generic service phrases such as "I hope this helps" and "Please don't hesitate to reach out" unless they suit the real relationship and purpose.

## Manufactured certainty

Review these shapes carefully:

- "The problem is not P, it is Q."
- "It is not about A, it is about B."
- "That is the real cost."
- "That is the whole problem."
- A short final sentence added only to sound decisive.
- Claims built on "nobody", "no one", "nothing" or "never" without direct evidence covering the full claim.

State the supported fact, its scope and its consequence. Keep an absolute only when the source or explicit rule establishes it.
