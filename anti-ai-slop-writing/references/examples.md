# Writing examples

Use these examples to recognise patterns, not as templates to copy. Preserve the facts and terminology of the real task. When a clear rewrite requires missing information, ask Benedict rather than filling the gap.

## Explain the problem and the proposed change

**Poor**

> The business runs as dozens of separate operations, one per salesperson, and what any of them knows leaves when they do.

**Better**

> Create one searchable source for product knowledge. Today, each salesperson relies on personal notes and experience, which makes that knowledge difficult for colleagues to find and retain when someone leaves.

**Why**

The first version uses a metaphor instead of naming the current practice, its consequence and the proposed change.

## Make commercial logic explicit

**Poor**

> Point marketing by demand, with margin as the tie-break.

**Better**

> Direct marketing spend towards destinations with the strongest customer demand. Where demand is similar, prioritise the destinations with higher margins.

**Why**

The rewrite replaces compressed shorthand with the action and decision rule. If the recommendation depends on missing cost data, explain that dependency in the next sentence.

## Resolve scope before improving the sentence

**Poor**

> Show the trip rather than describe it. Visual material for the call, at the point where interest is highest.

**Better when the scope is known**

> Give every sales team destination images and short videos to share during customer calls, so customers can see the proposed trip while deciding whether to proceed.

**Ask when the scope is unknown**

> Does this apply to every sales team, or only to the digital quote process?

**Why**

A polished sentence can still be wrong if the audience, channel or operating scope is unclear.

## Replace figurative instructions with the real action

**Poor**

> Speak to each market in its own idiom.

**Better**

> Adapt the marketing message for each country instead of reusing the UK wording. Use the local team's evidence on which offers and phrases perform well in that market.

**Why**

“Own idiom” could mean language, tone, offer design or campaign planning. The rewrite names the intended change.

## Use complete sentences in initiative descriptions

**Poor**

> Reporting people use, and the capacity to build it. Valuable work that goes unrequested stays unbuilt.

**Better**

> Give managers reports they can use without asking an analyst to rebuild them. Today, a small reporting team handles every request, so lower-priority reporting needs often wait.

**Why**

The first version contains a fragment and a compressed aphorism. The rewrite identifies the user, current constraint and practical consequence.

## Explain technical terms for a non-specialist reader

**Poor**

> Add an event-driven ingestion layer with idempotent consumers and a DLQ.

**Better**

> Process each incoming update automatically as it arrives. The service records which updates it has already handled, so a retry does not create duplicates, and sends failed updates to a separate queue for investigation.

**Why**

The technical terms may be correct, but an outside reader needs to know what the design does and why it matters. Use the technical name afterwards if the document needs it.

## Write external updates without corporate filler

**Poor**

> We have leveraged the available data to unlock a robust view of the opportunity landscape and will circle back on next steps.

**Better**

> We reviewed the available sales and margin data. It shows three opportunities worth testing; we will send the supporting analysis and proposed next steps on Friday.

**Why**

The rewrite states the work completed, the result and the promised action. It also avoids claiming more than the evidence shows.

## Limit claims to the evidence reviewed

**Poor**

> Nobody uses the current report because it never answers the questions the team has.

**Better**

> None of the six people interviewed uses the report regularly. They said it does not show performance by customer segment, so they export the data and rebuild that view in Excel.

**Why**

The first version turns limited evidence into a universal claim. The rewrite gives the observed scope, reason and current behaviour.

## Keep the tone modern without becoming chatty

**Poor**

> Here's the thing: the current process is a bit of a mess, so let's get everyone on the same page.

**Better**

> The current process differs between teams, which makes handovers difficult. Agreeing one process and recording the exceptions would make responsibilities clearer.

**Why**

The rewrite remains direct without slang, banter or a conversational performance.
